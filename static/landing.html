A shift takes about five minutes and nobody tells you the right order.

You get a role, a team and a clock. Prioritising under pressure is the skill being practised.

1

Read your brief

You are the new analyst. A private note says what you know and who you work with.

2

Work the huddle

Ask questions, push back, give people what they need. Teammates reply as they would at work.

3

Handle the twist

Halfway through, the situation changes. Your plan has to change with it.

4

Write your recommendation

Two to four sentences with figures. Then see how you did, and try again.

Example result
ClarityProficient
“How much cash do we have today, and what is owed to the supplier?”

Why: a specific question that points at the numbers that decide the answer.

CommunicationFoundation
“Just give her the discount, she is a good customer.”

Improve: say what it costs and when you will confirm delivery.

Feedback that shows its evidence.

  • Every judgement quotes youIf there is no quote to support a level, the result says there is not enough evidence.
  • Quotes are checked by codeA quote must exist in your messages before it is shown. Made-up evidence is rejected.
  • Try again and see the changeA second attempt uses fresh figures, so improvement is something you can show.

Employers see what you did, not what you claim.

Your Evidence Card is a link. You choose what appears on it, and you can switch it off any time.

  • Evidence over certificatesLevels come with your own words, so a reader can judge for themselves.
  • AI-assessed or human-verifiedEvery card says which one it is, and what the AI could not judge.

AI scores can be wrong

So every AI-scored result is labelled, and the top level is not awarded without a person checking.

It is practice, not a licence

A card shows what you did in one simulated shift. It does not replace experience.

Your data stays minimal

Names and contact details are removed before scoring, and you consent before anything starts.
